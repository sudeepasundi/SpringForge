## Why it matters

"How many orders last month?" "Revenue per category?" "Which customers spent more than 500?" Every
dashboard and report is an aggregation. Getting it right means knowing what the aggregate functions
count, how grouping works, and the difference between filtering rows and filtering groups.

## How it works

### Aggregate functions

An aggregate turns many rows into one value.

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="shop"
  query={`SELECT COUNT(*)             AS products,
       COUNT(DISTINCT category_id) AS categories_used,
       MIN(price)           AS cheapest,
       MAX(price)           AS dearest,
       ROUND(AVG(price), 2) AS average_price,
       SUM(stock)           AS units_in_stock
FROM product;`}
/>

### GROUP BY: one result row per group

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="shop"
  query={`SELECT status, COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY status
ORDER BY orders DESC;`}
/>

Group by several columns for finer groups, and join first to aggregate across tables:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="shop"
  title="Revenue per category from delivered orders"
  query={`SELECT c.name AS category,
       COUNT(DISTINCT o.id)                   AS orders,
       SUM(oi.quantity)                       AS units,
       ROUND(SUM(oi.quantity * oi.unit_price), 2) AS revenue
FROM order_item oi
JOIN orders o   ON o.id = oi.order_id
JOIN product p  ON p.id = oi.product_id
JOIN category c ON c.id = p.category_id
WHERE o.status = 'DELIVERED'
GROUP BY c.name
ORDER BY revenue DESC;`}
/>

### WHERE versus HAVING

- `WHERE` filters **rows** before grouping.
- `HAVING` filters **groups** after aggregation — it can use aggregates.

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="shop"
  title="Customers with at least 3 non-cancelled orders"
  query={`SELECT customer_id, COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE status <> 'CANCELLED'        -- rows: ignore cancelled orders
GROUP BY customer_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 3               -- groups: keep busy customers
ORDER BY orders DESC;`}
/>

Put conditions on plain columns in `WHERE`: fewer rows reach the grouping step, which is faster.

### Aggregating over an empty set

With no `GROUP BY`, an aggregate query always returns **one** row — even over zero rows. `COUNT` gives
`0`; `SUM`, `AVG`, `MIN` and `MAX` give `NULL`:

<SqlPlayground
  dataset="shop"
  query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS n, SUM(price) AS total
FROM product
WHERE price > 100000;`}
/>

Wrap with `COALESCE(SUM(x), 0)` when you need zero.

## Common mistakes

- Selecting a column that is neither grouped nor aggregated (see [How a Query Runs](/sql/how-a-query-runs)).
- `COUNT(column)` when you meant `COUNT(*)` — rows with a `NULL` are not counted.
- Summing after a join that multiplied rows (see [Joins](/sql/joins)).
- Averaging averages: the average of per-group averages is not the overall average.

## Exercises

<SqlExercise
  dataset="hr"
  prompt="For each department_id, return the number of employees and the average salary (as avg_salary), skipping employees without a department."
  solution={`SELECT department_id, COUNT(*) AS employees, AVG(salary) AS avg_salary
FROM employee
WHERE department_id IS NOT NULL
GROUP BY department_id;`}
  hint="WHERE removes the NULL department before GROUP BY."
/>

<SqlExercise
  dataset="shop"
  prompt="Find product_ids that have been ordered in more than 3 different orders. Return product_id and the number of orders."
  solution={`SELECT product_id, COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS orders
FROM order_item
GROUP BY product_id
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) > 3;`}
  hint="GROUP BY product_id, then HAVING on a COUNT."
/>

<SqlExercise
  dataset="shop"
  prompt="Return the average rating per product_id, rounded to 1 decimal place, only for products with at least 2 reviews."
  solution={`SELECT product_id, ROUND(AVG(rating), 1) AS avg_rating
FROM review
GROUP BY product_id
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 2;`}
/>
