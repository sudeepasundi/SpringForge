A Kafka cluster is unusually forgiving right up until it is not. It absorbs a dead broker without
anyone noticing, and then a disk fills at 2am and the whole thing stops accepting writes.

Four metrics predict almost every incident. Everything else is detail.

## The four that matter

`UnderReplicatedPartitions` is the single most valuable one. It goes above zero before almost every
Kafka incident: a broker under GC pressure, a slow disk, a network partition, a botched rolling
restart. Treating it as an early warning rather than an error is most of operating Kafka well.

<Callout type="prod">
Alert on **predicted** time-to-full for disks, not on a static threshold:

```promql
predict_linear(kafka_log_size_bytes[6h], 4 * 3600) > kafka_disk_total_bytes
```

Kafka fills disks smoothly and predictably, and then stops dead. A static 85% alert on a topic that
grows 3% an hour gives you five hours; on one that grows 30% an hour it gives you thirty minutes.
The prediction gives you the same warning in both cases — the cause-based alerting exception from
[module 10](/learn/observability/slos-alerting).
</Callout>

## Consumer lag

Lag is the difference between the log end offset and the consumer's committed offset. It is the
metric that tells you whether the system is keeping up, and the one most often misread.

```bash
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 \
  --describe --group inventory-service
```

<Terminal>
{`GROUP              TOPIC                   PARTITION  CURRENT-OFFSET  LOG-END-OFFSET  LAG
inventory-service  shopflow.order.events   0          1048221         1048224         3
inventory-service  shopflow.order.events   1          1047992         1048003         11
inventory-service  shopflow.order.events   2          892014          1048198         156184
inventory-service  shopflow.order.events   3          1048100         1048109         9`}
</Terminal>

That output is a specific diagnosis, not a general problem. Three partitions are current and
**partition 2 is 156,000 records behind** — so this is not "consumers are slow", it is one of:

- **A hot key.** One key with disproportionate volume lands on one partition, and no amount of
  scaling helps because a partition has exactly one consumer.
- **A stuck consumer.** The instance owning partition 2 is wedged — a lock, a hung HTTP call, an
  infinite retry.
- **A poison message** being retried forever, blocking everything behind it.

Total lag would have hidden all three behind an average.

<Callout type="tip" title="Alert on lag in time, not records">
"50,000 records behind" means nothing without a rate. At 10,000/second it is five seconds; at
10/second it is well over an hour.

Burrow and `kafka-lag-exporter` both compute lag in **seconds**, which is directly comparable to an
SLO and does not need reinterpreting when traffic changes.
</Callout>

## Rolling a cluster

Restarting brokers one at a time is routine, and doing it wrong causes an outage that looks like a
Kafka bug.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the cluster is fully healthy first">

`UnderReplicatedPartitions` must be **zero**. Restarting a broker while replicas are already behind
takes a partition below `min.insync.replicas` and stops writes.

```bash
kafka-topics.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 --describe --under-replicated-partitions
```

Empty output, or do not proceed.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Move leadership off the broker before stopping it">

Controlled shutdown does this, and it is on by default — but confirm it, because without it every
partition this broker leads becomes unavailable until a new leader is elected.

```properties
controlled.shutdown.enable=true
controlled.shutdown.max.retries=3
```

  </Step>
  <Step title="Restart, then wait for the ISR to recover">

The broker comes back with cold replicas and must catch up. Waiting for
`UnderReplicatedPartitions` to return to zero before touching the next broker is the entire
discipline — and the step that gets skipped under time pressure.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Rebalance leadership when you are done">

After a rolling restart, leadership is unevenly distributed — the last broker restarted leads almost
nothing. Preferred-replica election restores the balance.

```bash
kafka-leader-election.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 --election-type preferred --all-topic-partitions
```

  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="pitfall" title="auto.leader.rebalance.enable can cause the outage it prevents">
Automatic rebalancing moves leadership back to preferred replicas on a timer. On a large cluster that
can trigger a leadership storm during recovery — exactly when the cluster is least able to absorb it.

Many operators disable it and run preferred-replica election manually after maintenance. If you leave
it on, at least set `leader.imbalance.check.interval.seconds` high enough that it does not fire
mid-recovery.
</Callout>

## When a broker dies

The window marked in red is what makes this urgent rather than routine. With `replication.factor=3`
and `min.insync.replicas=2`, one broker down still accepts writes; two down does not. Recovery is
therefore a race, and the correct instinct is to restore replication quickly rather than to
investigate the root cause first.

Reassigning partitions to a replacement broker:

```bash
# 1. Generate a plan.
kafka-reassign-partitions.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 \
  --topics-to-move-json-file topics.json \
  --broker-list "1,3,4" --generate > plan.json

# 2. Execute it — throttled, or replication traffic starves live produce/consume.
kafka-reassign-partitions.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 \
  --reassignment-json-file plan.json --execute \
  --throttle 50000000        # 50 MB/s

# 3. Verify, and remove the throttle. Forgetting step 3 leaves the cluster
#    permanently throttled, which is a slow-burning mystery months later.
kafka-reassign-partitions.sh --bootstrap-server kafka:9092 \
  --reassignment-json-file plan.json --verify
```

<Callout type="prod">
The throttle is not optional on a busy cluster. Unthrottled reassignment saturates the network
between brokers and pushes healthy partitions out of the ISR — turning a one-broker problem into a
cluster-wide one.

And genuinely do run `--verify`. It is what removes the throttle. A cluster left throttled replicates
slowly forever, which presents months later as unexplained under-replication under load.
</Callout>

## Retention and disk, together

Disk pressure is the most common self-inflicted Kafka outage, and it is arithmetic:

```text
bytes/day × retention days × replication factor × (1 + headroom)
= disk needed

  40 GB/day × 14 days × 3 × 1.4 ≈ 2.4 TB across the cluster
```

Two things people forget in that sum: **replication factor multiplies everything**, and
[retention is a floor, not a ceiling](/learn/kafka-ops/topics-and-retention) — a partition holds an
unrolled active segment beyond its retention window.

When a disk does fill, the broker stops accepting writes for the affected log directories. Reducing
`retention.ms` on a large topic frees space within a cleaner cycle and is usually the fastest
recovery — faster than adding a disk, and far faster than deleting topics by hand.
