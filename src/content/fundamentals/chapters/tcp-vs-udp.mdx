## Why it matters

Most of what you build rides on TCP: HTTP/1.1 and 2, database connections, Kafka, Redis. Its behaviour
explains why the first request is slower than the rest, why connection pools exist, why a server can
run out of ports under load, and why video calls and HTTP/3 chose UDP instead.

### The three-way handshake

Both sides agree on starting sequence numbers, which they then use to number every byte. With
round trips of 80 ms to another continent, the handshake alone costs 80 ms — and TLS adds one or two
more round trips. **That is why connections are reused** (HTTP keep-alive, connection pools).

### How TCP makes delivery reliable

- **Sequence numbers** let the receiver put bytes back in order and drop duplicates.
- **Acknowledgements** (ACKs) tell the sender what arrived.
- **Retransmission**: anything not acknowledged within a timeout is sent again.
- **Checksums** detect corrupted segments.

### Flow and congestion control

- **Flow control** protects the *receiver*: it advertises a window — "I can take this many more bytes".
- **Congestion control** protects the *network*: the sender starts slowly (**slow start**), grows its
  rate while everything is acknowledged, and backs off sharply when packets are lost. A new connection
  is therefore slower than a warmed-up one.

**Head-of-line blocking**: because TCP delivers a single ordered stream, one lost packet stalls
everything behind it until it is retransmitted. HTTP/2 multiplexes many requests over one TCP
connection, so one loss delays all of them — the reason HTTP/3 moved to QUIC over UDP.

### Closing, and TIME_WAIT

A connection closes with FIN and ACK in each direction. The side that closes first stays in
**TIME_WAIT** for a while (commonly 60 seconds on Linux) so stray late packets are not mistaken for a
new connection. A client that opens and closes thousands of short connections per second can pile up
TIME_WAIT sockets and run out of ephemeral ports.

### Other states worth recognising

- **SYN_SENT** stuck: the server did not answer — firewall or host down (you will see a connect timeout).
- **CLOSE_WAIT** piling up: *your* application received the other side's close but never closed its
  socket — a resource leak (an unclosed HTTP response or stream).
- **RST** (reset): the other side refused or aborted — "connection reset by peer".

### When UDP wins

UDP makes sense when **fresh beats complete** (a late video frame is useless), when messages are tiny
and independent (a DNS lookup), or when the application implements its own reliability on top — which
is exactly what **QUIC** does, adding encryption, multiplexing without head-of-line blocking, and fast
connection set-up.

## When you'll meet it

- **"The first call is slow, then it is fast."** Handshakes (TCP + TLS) and connection pool warm-up.
- **"Connect timed out" versus "read timed out."** The first fails during the handshake; the second
  after connecting, while waiting for a response. Configure both.
- **Thousands of CLOSE_WAIT sockets.** Responses or streams are not being closed.
- **Load balancer idle timeout shorter than the pool's keep-alive.** The pool reuses a connection the
  load balancer already dropped, producing occasional "connection reset" errors.
- **Interview:** "TCP versus UDP?" and "Explain the three-way handshake."
