## Why it matters

Knowing how to find answers — and how to ask for help when you can't — multiplies everything else you
know. A well-asked question gets a fast, useful answer; a vague one gets ignored or starts a long
back-and-forth. Often, preparing a good question answers it.

## How it works

### Read the error message — all of it

Error messages usually say exactly what went wrong. Read the **first line** (the exception and message),
the **first frame in your code**, and any **`Caused by:`** further down.

```text
org.springframework.beans.factory.UnsatisfiedDependencyException:
  Error creating bean with name 'orderService' …
Caused by: NoSuchBeanDefinitionException:
  No qualifying bean of type 'com.shop.PaymentGateway' available
```

The real problem is in the last `Caused by`: nothing implements `PaymentGateway` as a bean — a missing
`@Component`, or a missing dependency.

### Search well

- Search the **exact error message**, in quotes, minus your own names and values.
- Add the technology and **version**: `"NoSuchBeanDefinitionException" spring boot 3`.
- Check the **date and version** of any answer you find — a 2014 answer may describe a different API.
- Prefer official docs, issue trackers and release notes over random blogs.

### Read documentation properly

- **Guides** show how to do common tasks.
- **Reference** documents every option and its default — the place to check exact behaviour.
- **Javadoc** tells you what a method promises, what it throws, and its thread-safety.
- **Changelogs** explain why something that used to work doesn't.

### Ask a question that gets answered

"Hi, my Spring app is not working, can anyone help?"

**Tried:** added `cascade = CascadeType.ALL` — no change. Checked the items list is non-empty before saving (logged: 3 items).

**Minimal example:** the two entity classes and the 10-line service method below.

</Good>
</Compare>

A useful question contains:

1. What you are trying to do (the goal, not just the step you are stuck on).
2. What you expected, and what happened instead — with the exact error.
3. What you tried, and what you learned from it.
4. Versions and environment.
5. A **minimal reproducible example**.

### The minimal reproducible example

Cut your problem down to the **smallest code that still shows it**: one class, one test, no unrelated
dependencies. Making it is the single best debugging technique there is — halfway through removing
unrelated code, you usually find the cause yourself. And if you don't, anyone can now reproduce it in
two minutes.

### Asking colleagues

- **Time-box first** (see [When You Are Stuck](/craft/when-you-are-stuck)), then ask — waiting two days to avoid
  "bothering" people costs the team more.
- Ask in a **shared channel** rather than a private message where possible; others learn from the answer.
- Say what you already tried, so nobody suggests it again.
- **Close the loop**: post what fixed it. Next time someone searches, they find the answer.
