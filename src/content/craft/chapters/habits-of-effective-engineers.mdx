## Why it matters

Two developers with similar technical skill can have very different impact. The difference is usually
habits: how they break down work, estimate, communicate, protect their focus and keep learning. None of
these are taught in a degree, and all of them can be practised.

## How it works

### Break work down before you start

A ticket like "add order tracking" becomes a list of small, checkable tasks:

```markdown
- [ ] Add `tracking_number` and `carrier` columns (migration)
- [ ] Store tracking info from the shipping webhook
- [ ] GET /orders/{id}/tracking endpoint + tests
- [ ] Show tracking link on the order page
- [ ] Email the customer when tracking is available
```

Each task is a few hours at most and can often be its own small pull request. You always know what is
next, progress is visible, and surprises show up early.

### Estimate honestly

- Estimate the small tasks, not the whole feature — and add them up.
- Include the parts people forget: tests, review, fixing review comments, deployment, documentation.
- Give a **range** with your confidence: "2–3 days; the webhook format is the unknown."
- When you learn something that changes the estimate, **say so early**. A delay announced on day 1 is a
  plan; announced on the due date, it is a problem.

### Write things down

- **A work log**: what you did, what you learned, what is blocked. It makes stand-ups easy, helps with
  performance reviews, and remembers what you don't.
- **Decisions and reasons** in pull requests, tickets or short design notes — "we chose X because Y".
- **Runbooks and READMEs** for anything you had to figure out the hard way.

### Communicate status

"Tracking: webhook storage done and merged. Endpoint in review. Blocked on the carrier's test account —

Say what is done, what is next, and what is blocked — and escalate blockers early.

### Protect your focus

- Deep work needs long uninterrupted blocks; batch messages and meetings where you can.
- Keep a single list of tasks, so remembering things doesn't occupy your head.
- Finish before starting: three half-done tasks deliver nothing.
- Leave a note to yourself at the end of the day — "next: handle the 404 case" — so tomorrow starts
  quickly.

### Own the outcome

- **Test it in a realistic environment**, not only on your machine.
- Watch it after release: logs, metrics, errors. Shipping is not the end of the task.
- When something breaks, fix it, then ask how to prevent that kind of failure — without blaming anyone.
