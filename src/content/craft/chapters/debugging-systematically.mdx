## Why it matters

Debugging by guessing — change something, run it, change something else — can take hours and often
"fixes" the symptom while the cause stays. Debugging systematically is the scientific method applied to
code: observe, form a hypothesis, test it, narrow down. It is slower for the first five minutes and much
faster overall.

## The method

<Steps>
  <Step title="Reproduce it reliably">

Find exact steps, inputs and environment that make the bug happen **every time**. If you can't
reproduce it, you can't know you've fixed it. Turn the reproduction into a failing automated test if
you can.

</Step>
  <Step title="Describe expected versus actual">

"Expected total 90.00 for a 100.00 order with a 10% coupon; got 100.00." Precise statements point at
precise causes.

</Step>
  <Step title="Gather evidence">

Read the **whole** error and stack trace — the first line of *your* code in it, and any `Caused by:`.
Check logs around the time. Look at the actual data.

</Step>
  <Step title="Form a hypothesis">

"The coupon isn't loaded" or "it's loaded but applied after the total is saved". Something specific and
testable.

</Step>
  <Step title="Test the hypothesis">

Add a log line, set a breakpoint, or write a tiny test that proves or disproves it. One change at a time.

</Step>
  <Step title="Narrow down, then fix the cause">

Repeat until you find where the behaviour first goes wrong. Fix the **root cause**, not the place the
symptom appears, and keep the failing test so it never comes back.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Reading a stack trace

```text
java.lang.NullPointerException: Cannot invoke "com.shop.Coupon.percent()" because "coupon" is null
    at com.shop.order.PriceCalculator.applyCoupon(PriceCalculator.java:42)
    at com.shop.order.PriceCalculator.totalFor(PriceCalculator.java:18)
    at com.shop.order.OrderService.place(OrderService.java:31)
    at com.shop.order.OrderController.place(OrderController.java:25)
    … 87 more (Spring, Tomcat)
```

- Read the **message** first: which reference was null.
- The **top frame in your code** is where it blew up (line 42); the frames below show how you got there.
- Ignore framework frames at first; they are almost never the cause.
- Look for **`Caused by:`** — the real exception is often at the bottom of a chain.

## Seeing it in practice

<Walkthrough
  title="The discount that sometimes disappears"
  problem="Customers report that their 10% coupon is sometimes not applied. It works when you test it by hand."
>
  <Stage title="Reproduce" prompt="'Sometimes' and 'works for me' — what do you do first?">

Collect failing examples: order ids from the support tickets. Compare them with successful ones. Pattern:
every failing order has **more than one coupon field** set in the request — the mobile app sends both
`coupon` and `couponCode`. Now it reproduces every time with that request body.

</Stage>
  <Stage title="Hypothesise" prompt="What might be going on with two coupon fields?">

Hypothesis: the request DTO maps `couponCode`, but the mobile app's `coupon` field arrives last and some
mapping step overwrites the value with null.

</Stage>
  <Stage title="Test the hypothesis" prompt="What is the fastest way to confirm or reject it?">

Write a test posting both fields and set a breakpoint in the controller. The DTO has `couponCode =
"SAVE10"` — the hypothesis is **wrong**: mapping is fine. That's still progress: one whole area is ruled out.

Next hypothesis: something downstream picks the other field. Stepping further shows
`CouponResolver.resolve(request)` reads `request.coupon()` **first**, which the mobile app sends as an
empty string, and `""` is treated as "no coupon".

</Stage>
  <Stage title="Fix the cause" prompt="Where is the right place to fix it, and how do you prove it?">

Root cause: an empty string treated as a real value, plus two fields for one concept. Fix: treat blank as
absent (`isBlank()`), and deprecate the duplicate field in the API. Keep the failing test — now passing —
plus one for the blank case. The symptom (missing discount) was far from the cause (field precedence).

</Stage>
</Walkthrough>

## Habits that save hours

- **Change one thing at a time**, and undo changes that didn't help.
- **Question your assumptions**: "that can't be null" — check it.
- **Read the error message.** Really read it, all of it.
- **Explain it to someone** (or a rubber duck) — the gap often shows up mid-sentence.
- **Take a break** after 30–60 minutes of no progress.
- **Write down what you tried**, so you don't retry it and can hand over cleanly.
